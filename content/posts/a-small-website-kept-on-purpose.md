title: LLMs and the paradigm shift of technology
date: 2026-11-01

---

Products built around large language models are becoming the new front door to the internet.

### Google versus AI labs

Instead of using the Google or DuckDuckGo websites, I increasingly find documents like developer blogs and medical procedure guidelines through ChatGPT.

Current AI search capabilities are still very much in the early days.

They feel like a more efficient search through Google, due to models being trained with "important" people in their field, or references to their blogs through Reddit or other blogs.

Different search indexes like Firecrawl (or even Reddit) that require payment to access their files seems like an extremely efficient way to keep this going, where their primary interface is a model's context.

### Agent behavior while searching Reddit

For an experiment to observe AI agent search behavior, I downloaded 3 months of Reddit posts and comments from the r/LocalLlama subreddit, to observe the search behavior of GPT 6.1 Sol.

When given questions to answer from the data, it was able to write task-specific Python code to make regex searches against keywords it came up with from the conversation's context.

The code was incredibly convoluted, compressed into a single bash command that piped the raw, unchecked Python code into the python interpreter at hand.

But it worked great, fetching me incredibly information-dense and opinionated comments from Reddit users who were deep in the weeds of experimenting with their own hardware setups.

A comparable search ran on default Google AI search returned substantially worse results, taken from a variety of SEO-marketed websites and YouTube videos of varying usefulness.

Having access to that sweet, sweet Reddit data made the specific searches I made significantly more performant, which makes me wonder whether companies or projects specializing in search can carve out a niche for themselves.

The big labs are only as powerful as their own proprietary data, and their search tools.

### Old search deteriorating

The internet superhighway of old is cluttered with bots and decaying search algorithms.

Recently, I searched up "Alan Watts lecture" on YouTube as part of a morning Yoga exercise.

Most of the results seemed like "slop", mass-produced AI content designed to take advantage of social media creator monetization arbitrages.

Instead of going to Google, I decided to ask ChatGPT for a good Alan Watts lecture to watch, and it returned an appropriate video that I found suitable.

YouTube and Google search *feel* like they have deteriorated significantly over the past year.

Both companies have not effectively managing SEO and automated posting accounts, leading to a general feeling of finding it harder to find original and useful content/information online.

This is very bad for the health of the internet, as those two companies make up a vast percentage of the way people interact with the internet.

### Paid private search

I can see how new projects allowing users to experience the internet directly through model search providers can improve the capabilities of LLMs, which then improves the experience of using the models from an AI lab.

Firecrawl is an interesting project, aimed at providing unique search capabilities for any LLM, and has yielded promising results from my tinkering with it so far.

I have not yet benchmarked whether or not Claude is better with Firecrawl than without, as Claude is occasionally very capable for creative project-building using Anthropic's own search tooling.

However, the survivability of projects like Firecrawl depend on whether they will be able to keep ahold of their proprietary data, fending off "data exfiltration" efforts, as the value of organic, human-made data continues to increase.
